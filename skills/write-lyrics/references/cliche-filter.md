# Cliché Filter — AI Lyric Defaults

Observed patterns that make AI-generated lyrics sound interchangeable. Use as a **flag list**, not an auto-kill list. Concrete, earned uses stay; defaults get rewritten.

Source basis: cross-song frequency analysis of AI music outputs (scenery nouns, rhyme shelves, stock phrases, structural devices). Curate and extend for your own catalog over time — add rows when you notice yourself repeating a move.

---

## Banned surface tokens

Replace with concrete, sensory specifics — a thing, a place, a brand, a year:

neon / neon lights / neon cities, echoes, symphony, shadows, intertwined / entwined, melody (as metaphor, not the music itself), spectral, whispers / whispered dreams, crimson, "with every [step/breath/touch/move]", come what may, hand in hand, electric glow / digital glow, grace, embrace, soar, rise above, pulse, chasing dreams, baby (as filler address), forever, heart, soul, eyes, tears, stars, fire, rain, light, heaven, together, pain, mind, mystery, collide, unfold, tangled sheets.

Not every use of "light" or "hands" is wrong — the problem is **default scenery** with no named anchor.

## Over-used scenery nouns

Replace with a specifically-named place, object, brand, or year before reusing as abstract scenery:

`ground`, `door`, `room`, `floor`, `dark`, `fire`, `world`, `morning`, `car`, `hands`, `light`, `sound`

Avoid the bare constructions: "the ground," "the dark," "the world," "the door," "the fire," "in the dark," "in the fire."

Prefer: a *kitchen counter*, a *back porch in August*, a *'07 Civic*, a *gas station off I-95*, a *screen door*, a *grandmother's couch*.

## Over-used imagery / scenarios

- Coffee / mornings (fine when authentic to the writer — never as the automatic opener)
- Mirrors as emotional shorthand
- Driving, hand on the wheel
- Glowing phone screens
- The "maybe X just…" hedge/realization construction

## First-person rut phrases

No two verses in a row may open with the same construction. Vary syntax (imperative, second person, fragment, question, dropped pronoun):

`I learned how to`, `I still`, `I never`, `I know`, `I want`, `I'm scared of`, `Maybe I`

## Catalog rhyme-shelf

Try slant, internal, or no rhyme before locking these as line-end words:

`know`, `fast`, `now`, `go`, `back`, `down`, `land`, `way`, `out`, `light`, `sound`, `knew`, `ground`

The **down / sound / ground** stack is the strongest crutch — explicitly avoid.

## Lazy end-rhyme pairs

Reach for slant, internal, or no rhyme first:

fire/desire, love/above, heart/start, night/light, girl/world, cry/die, pain/rain, dream/seem, eyes/skies, you/true, baby/crazy, life/wife, mind/find, kiss/bliss, dance/romance, feel/real, fly/high, tear/fear, star/far, blue/you, town/down, all/fall, night/fight, home/alone, true/blue, away/stay, again/pain.

## Trope traps

If the song's center *is* one of these, ground it with a specific person/place/object/year so it isn't *only* the trope:

unrequited love, generic heartbreak, partying/clubbing romance, "soulmates / forever love," generic struggles & perseverance, triumph over adversity, run-away-from-it-all, idealized beauty, rebellious outsider, "good old days" nostalgia, underdog story, YOLO, the journey (literal or metaphorical), longing for freedom, finding myself, yearning for a better future.

## Structural devices already overused

Don't reuse without a specific reason this song needs the exact device:

- "Lost in the X" chorus refrain
- "I'm not scared of X / I'm scared of Y" parallel bridge
- "Let it X, let it Y" chorus stack
- "Maybe X, maybe Y" speculative opener
- "Things are changing / We'll be okay" tag
- "Round and round" chorus opener

## Self-audit checklist (final pass)

- [ ] Any line resolves in a universal abstraction with no concrete anchor in the same stanza?
- [ ] Chorus ends on a cliché hook (forever, together, hold me, never let go)?
- [ ] Cosmic/elemental stacking (stars + fire + sky + rain + light + heaven) — cut at least two?
- [ ] Two verses open with the same construction?
- [ ] Could this song have been written by anyone, about anyone?
- [ ] Rhyme-shelf words or lazy pairs used as easy exits?
